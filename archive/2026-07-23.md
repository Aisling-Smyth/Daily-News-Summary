## 🇮🇪 Ireland

### MI5, PSNI to pay damages for accessing journalist's phone

MI5 agents and officers from the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) have agreed to pay damages to journalist Vincent Kearney following a legal case alleging unlawful access to his mobile phone data. A court ruled that MI5 operatives accessed Mr. Kearney’s device in 2018, obtaining detailed information about his communications. 

The ruling highlights concerns regarding state surveillance practices and the protection of journalistic sources. The case raises questions about oversight and accountability surrounding intelligence agencies’ activities, particularly with regard to accessing personal data without proper legal authorization. It is likely to fuel ongoing debates about civil liberties and government transparency in the digital age.

### Ex-DUP leader Jeffrey Donaldson stripped of knighthood

Former Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) leader Jeffrey Donaldson has been removed from the King’s Privy Council and consequently stripped of his Knight Grand Cross of the Order of St Michael and St George title. This action was taken by British King Charles III following Donaldson’s conviction on six counts of having prohibited sexual abuse offences against vulnerable children.

The removal reflects a significant distancing between the British monarchy and the DUP, a party historically closely associated with traditional conservative values. It also highlights the ongoing legal challenges facing Donaldson and underscores broader concerns regarding historical abuse within Northern Ireland, potentially impacting future royal associations and scrutiny of individuals holding positions of public trust.

### Two gardaí hospitalised after processing seized vape products

Two Garda officers have been hospitalised following an incident while processing a large quantity of seized vaping products. The officers became unwell after handling the products, and were subsequently treated in St. James’s Hospital in Dublin.

The incident highlights concerns regarding the potential health risks associated with illicit e-cigarettes and raises questions about safety protocols for law enforcement dealing with confiscated goods. It also underscores ongoing efforts to combat the proliferation of unregulated vaping products within Ireland.
